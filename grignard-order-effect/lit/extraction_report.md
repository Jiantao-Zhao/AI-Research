# Extracted facts — Peltzer 2017 (JPCB 121, 4226); Peltzer/Gauss/Eisenstein/Cascella 2020 (JACS 142, 2984)

References are to `Peltzer2017_JPCB_Schlenk.txt` and `JACS2020_GrignardPuzzle.txt`. Both are two-column extractions, so some sentences span successive lines (span given). Figures are images, so only numbers repeated in prose exist here; "not stated" = absent from these files.

## 1. Peltzer 2017 — CH₃MgCl/THF, AIMD + metadynamics

**a. Setup.** CP2K 2.5.1/QUICKSTEP (L165–166); PBE (L190–191); GPW basis, DZVP (first/second row, Mg), "molecularly optimized basis set for the chlorine atoms", 200 Ry cutoff, GTH pseudopotentials, D3 dispersion (L192–202); Born–Oppenheimer dynamics, Δt 0.25 fs, 10⁻⁵ au gradient (L202–204). Monomers: 1 CH₃MgCl, MgCl₂ or Mg(CH₃)₂ + **25 THF** in **15.0 × 15.0 × 15.0 Å³** (L136–139); dimers in **25.2 × 15.0 × 15.0 Å³, 42 THF** (L157–158); finite-size check box 25.2 × 20.0 × 20.0 Å³, 74 THF (L158–159). 300 K: 15 ps microcanonical relaxation, CSVR (τ = 10 fs), production **NVT with Nosé–Hoover chain (3, τ = 1 ps)** (L143–152). Monomers run "over 40 ps" (L201). **Metadynamics and dimerization run lengths are not stated in the main text** (Table S1, L158, L237). All CVs are coordination numbers CN[X](t) of a ligand to a given Mg (eq 1, L211–233); Gaussians **0.25 kcal mol⁻¹ high, 0.04 wide, every 50 steps** — stated "for the dichloride bridged system" only (L234–236). Free energies are read off the metadynamics FES, validated by reaching the diffusion limit inside the walls, statistical errors "within 1−3kT" (L160–164). Static checks: PBE+GD3/6-31+G**, Gaussian 09, SMD, Gibbs at 298.15 K/1 atm, 1 M (24.465 atm) for bimolecular steps (L169–188).

**b. Free energies (kcal mol⁻¹).** No overall ΔG for 2 CH₃MgCl → Mg(CH₃)₂ + MgCl₂ is quoted. Reported: MgCl₂(THF)₃ lies "∼1.6 and ∼2.8" below the 2- and 4-THF forms (L245–248); dimerization "slightly endoergic by 4.9" (static, L531–536); **D^ClCl_11 = 0, D^ClCl_22 +1.3, D^ClCl_12 +2.7** (~1% of room-temperature samples), ≥3 THF on one Mg >5 (L336–357); interchange among D^ClCl_11/12/22 "<5" (L273–281, L536); D^ClCl "more stable by ∼3.5" than D^ClMe (L314–316); **A→C ≈ 8, C→D ≈ 6, and B→D "13 kcal mol⁻¹ higher than that of well A"** (L321–323); D^ClMe_11 = 0, D^ClMe_21 and D^ClMe^THF_11 **+3**, D^ClMe_12 **+5**, split by **0.5** over a "2kT" barrier, exchange barriers ~4, bridging→terminal rearrangement **<0.5** (L405–426, L589–615); product release **−8.7** to CH₃MgCl(THF)₂ + MgCl₂(THF)₃ (L584–587). Abstract: "The energy difference between all dinuclear solvated structures is lower than 5 kcal mol⁻¹" (L40–41).

**c. Solvation.** CH₃MgCl and Mg(CH₃)₂ carry only **2 THF**, tetrahedral Mg (L201–205); MgCl₂ samples 4- and 5-coordinate states, 3-THF trigonal-bipyramidal most stable (L224–233). "At room temperature, the different chemical species involved in the reaction accept multiple solvation structures, with two to four THF molecules that can coordinate the Mg atoms" (L37–39). Transfer always leaves from an axial site: "the transferred ligands are always departing from an axial position of a pentacoordinated Mg atom" (L46–47).

**d. Timescale / dynamic character.** **No rate constant, ps lifetime, or rate law appears anywhere.** Qualitative only: interchange is "fast … at room temperature" (L273–281), and D^ClCl_12 is "expected to be statistically present in 1% of room-temperature samples" (L348–352). Explicitly dynamic, not static: bond breaking at the most solvated Mg, bond making at the least solvated (L47–48), with "the dynamic exchange of solvent molecules in the Mg coordination sphere" declared key (L124–126).

**e. Conclusions/caveats.** "The reaction goes via the formation of a dinuclear (CH₃)Mg(μ-Cl)₂Mg(CH₃) intermediate whose most stable solvated form (with a single THF at each Mg) is unreactive" (L646–649); the tetracoordinate species favoured by earlier work "are indeed the most stable structures but are not on the reactive pathway" (L598–604). Caveats: only monomers/dinuclear species, higher aggregation excluded because THF association is low (L196–199); "The species selected by the dynamics to be on the reaction pathway are not necessarily minima on the PES" (L672–674).

## 2. Peltzer et al. 2020

**a. Systems/computational.** **CH₃MgCl in THF against two substrates: acetaldehyde (ACA) and fluorenone** (L31–36, L192–199). Each reactive complex made by "replacing one THF coordinated to Mg with a substrate molecule" in **25.2 × 15.0 × 15.0 Å³, 41 THF** (L136–140). PBE, same basis/pseudopotential/D3 protocol, Δt 0.25 fs, same thermostats (L139–182). ΔA‡ from **blue-moon constrained dynamics** on the CH₃···C(carbonyl) distance: nine λ points 0.2–0.1 Å apart, ⟨f⟩λ converged to ~0.5 kcal mol⁻¹ Å⁻¹ within ~2 ps, integrated trapezoidally to ⟨f⟩λ = 0; Helmholtz free energies (L184–199). BDEs: M06-2X/6-31+G(d,p) + SMD, Gaussian 09, benchmarked to CCSD(T)/cc-pVTZ in CFOUR (L200–213).

**b. Activation energies ΔA‡ (kcal mol⁻¹).** The full panel is Figure 2 (image), so prose values may be incomplete: **Agem 8.7 ± 0.8; Bgem 6.5 ± 0.7; Egem "similar" to Bgem (no number); Cgem and Dgem "both around 13"; Dbrd 22.6; Fvic ∼4.8; Cvic ∼13.0** (L217–233, L285–286, L316–317). **Fvic is most reactive overall** — "the lowest activation energy that has been calculated for all mononuclear and dinuclear species considered in this study" (L305–307), ~8 below Cvic and ~3.9 below Agem. The abstract names "a dinuclear Mg complex in which the substrate and the nucleophile initially bind to different Mg centers and the mononuclear dimethyl magnesium" (L38–40). Reason: Fvic's two pentacoordinate Mg (Mg1 = 1 THF + ACA; Mg2 = 2 THF) need no bulk THF at the TS, giving a favourable entropy term (L340–347, L373–377). Bridging methyl is worst: "a nucleophile is less reactive at a bridging than at a terminal position" (L232–233).

**c. Radical pathway.** Condition: "The radical reaction, which requires the homolytic cleavage of the Mg−CH₃ bond, cannot occur unless a substrate with a low-lying π*(CO) orbital coordinates the Mg center" (L40–41). **No radical TS barrier is computed**; the route is quantified by BDEs plus an assumed −TΔS. BDEs: **66.6 (n = 2) and 66.4 (n = 3)** for CH₃MgCl(THF)n vs **∼61** experimental (CH₃MgBr/Et₂O) (L419–426); **37.9** with ACA, **24.8** with carbonyl difluoride (L394–396); **16.5** for CH₃MgCl(fluorenone)(THF)₂, "more than 20 kcal mol⁻¹ smaller than that for ACA" (L450–452); Mg(CH₃)₂ lowers the BDE "by roughly 10%" (L423–426). With −TΔS "on the order of 10", radical formation from fluorenone costs "only ∼6 kcal mol⁻¹" (L461–470). Spin: 0.5 e on the fluorenone C=O carbon vs >0.8 e elsewhere, 33/16/45% on C/O/ring, ~0.8 e on Mg in MgCl(THF)₂• (L387–388, L411–412, L455–460). π*(CO) energies −0.18057 (ACA), −0.18846 (HCHO), −0.25703 au (COF₂) (L405–406). Nucleophilic fluorenone: **14.5 ± 1.2**, ~8 above ACA (L475–477).

**d. Ensemble quotes.** "The similar reactivity of several species in fast equilibrium implies that the reaction does not occur via a single process but by an ensemble of parallel reactions" (L44–45). "The coexistence of multiple species in rapid exchange due to the Schlenk equilibrium and the evidence that the activation energy range of all possible reactions is relatively modest indicate that the Grignard reaction should not be described by an individual process. Instead, it should rather be thought of as an ensemble of transformations that can occur simultaneously in solution" (L511–523). **No population weighting is computed** — abundances are imported from 2017 (Figure 6 caption, L301–303).

**e. Unknowns.** No future-work section. Explicit: "the mechanism of the Grignard reaction remains unresolved" (L21–22); reasons additives help "remain unclear" (L67–68); "no experimental study was able to assign the relative reactivity of the various forms of the organomagnesium species" (L84–86). Forward-looking: mapping "all possible reaction pathways … and the key factors regulating them" is the "foundation for rational improvements" (L528–535). Fluorenone was run only on Mg(CH₃)₂, chosen because it "allows faster ab initio MD simulations" (L445–449) — dinuclear fluorenone pathways were not computed.

2. A→C ~8, C→D ~6, B→D +13 (Peltzer2017 L321–323).

7. BDE: 66.6/66.4 free reagent, 37.9 ACA, 24.8 COF₂, 16.5 fluorenone (JACS2020 L394–396, L419–426, L450).
8. Fluorenone ΔA‡ 14.5 ± 1.2; net radical cost ~6 (JACS2020 L470, L476).
